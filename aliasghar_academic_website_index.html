Selected research

Master’s thesis · 2024–2026

Multimodal EEG–sEMG Fusion for Hand Gesture Recognition

Designed and recorded synchronized 32-channel EEG (g.tec; 512 Hz) and six-channel surface EMG (Noraxon; 2,000 Hz) from eight healthy participants performing four hand/wrist gestures. Developed a pipeline for filtering, ICA-based artifact reduction, time alignment, segmentation, and wavelet-coherence-based channel selection. Trained parallel CNN encoders with self-attention and cross-attention using TensorFlow/Keras.

95.68%Within-subject cross-validation accuracy
92.45%Task-based holdout accuracy
8Healthy research participants

The multimodal model retained 78.05% and 75.46% accuracy under the strongest simulated time-domain and frequency-domain muscle-fatigue perturbations, respectively; results are not clinical or cross-subject validation.

sEMG study · 2026

Fatigue-Resistant Hand Gesture Recognition

Compared conventional classifiers, CNNs, GRUs, and multi-head self-attention models on 250 ms sEMG windows from the same experimental recording sessions as the multimodal study (not a second separately collected dataset). MHA+CNN attained 98.23% clean-data accuracy. Under the strongest simulated spectral degradation, MHA+GRU retained 84.58% compared with 53.81% for the CNN baseline.

Undergraduate research · Summer 2023

Microsaccade Representation in the Visual Nervous System

Investigated microsaccades—small, involuntary eye movements—in rodents through recorded gaze data, identified microsaccadic events, and analyzed how visual image statistics were associated with their occurrence.
