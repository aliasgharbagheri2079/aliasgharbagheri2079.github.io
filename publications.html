Fatigue-Resistant s-EMG Hand Gesture Recognition with Lightweight Recurrent–Attention Networks

[1] A. Bagheri Tirtashi and M. Jahed, “Fatigue-Resistant s-EMG Hand Gesture Recognition with Lightweight Recurrent–Attention Networks,” in Proc. 34th International Conference on Electrical Engineering (ICEE), 2026, doi: 10.1109/ICEE70628.2026.11710601.

Research highlight: Studied whether compact sequence models with multi-head self-attention can maintain reliable sEMG-based gesture recognition when muscle-fatigue-like distortions alter signal amplitude and frequency content.

  • Research question: How robust are lightweight CNN- and GRU-based gesture classifiers when signals differ from the clean recordings used for training?
  • Data and methods: Six forearm sEMG channels from eight healthy participants performing four hand/wrist gestures. Compared classical feature-based classifiers with CNN, GRU, CNN+multi-head attention, and GRU+multi-head attention models on 250-ms windows.
  • Key findings: MHA+CNN achieved 98.23% accuracy on clean signals. Under the strongest simulated amplitude degradation, MHA+GRU retained 65.19% versus 29.21% for CNN; under the strongest spectral tilt, MHA+GRU retained 84.58% versus 53.81% for CNN.
  • Research relevance: The results support investigating lightweight temporal attention models for reliable myoelectric interfaces and rehabilitation devices.
  • Important limitation: Fatigue was simulated on non-fatigued recordings rather than measured from physically fatigued participants. Evaluation was subject-specific, not clinical or cross-subject validation.

Robust EEG–sEMG Fusion for Hand Gesture Recognition Using Cross-Attention

[2] A. Bagheri Tirtashi and M. Jahed, “Robust EEG–sEMG Fusion for Hand Gesture Recognition Using Cross-Attention,” 33rd International Conference on Biomedical Engineering (ICBME), 2026, accepted for publication.

Research highlight: Proposed a cross-attention EEG–sEMG fusion model that explicitly learns relationships between neural and muscular representations, aiming to improve hand-gesture decoding when muscular signals are degraded.

Explore research highlights, methodology, results and limitations
  • Research question: Can EEG provide complementary information that improves sEMG-based hand gesture recognition, especially under fatigue-related signal disturbances?
  • Data and methods: Simultaneously recorded 32-channel EEG and six-channel sEMG from eight healthy participants performing four gestures. Applied filtering, artifact reduction and synchronization, then used parallel CNN encoders, self-attention, and cross-attention for multimodal feature fusion.
  • Key findings: The proposed fusion model reached approximately 92.45% accuracy on clean data. At the strongest simulated time-domain attenuation, it retained 78.05%; at the strongest frequency-domain degradation, it retained 75.46%. Both were higher than the evaluated sEMG-only and alternative fusion baselines under the same conditions.
  • Research relevance: Cross-modal attention can exploit complementary cortical and muscular information for potentially more robust rehabilitation-oriented interfaces.
  • Important limitation: Muscle fatigue was represented by controlled sEMG signal perturbations, not a physiological fatigue experiment. Results do not establish clinical or cross-subject generalization.
